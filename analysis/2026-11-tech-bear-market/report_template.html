Tech Sell-Off Buy Plan

Is it time to buy the 25 tech stocks now in bear-market territory, and which ones? This note looks at the macro picture, valuations, history and risk to give a buying plan for $1,000 of new money, plus what to do with your AMD and Nebius shares.

Investor: low risk tolerance · 6-month horizon · shares only · no crypto names Already holds: AMD (cost $400) · Nebius (cost $200)
The decision Start buying, but in four steps

Yesterday looked like a panic flush: chip stocks had their second-worst day ever, and the fear gauge hit "extreme fear". The businesses themselves are still growing fast, and the five names below now trade at 15 to 19 times next year's earnings. That is cheap for this group. The macro picture has not turned, though. The Fed is still raising rates and selling hit Taiwan again this morning. Nobody can call the exact low, so spread the $1,000 over five weeks and keep $200 back in case prices fall much further.

Hold

AMD: you're about breakeven. Keep it, but don't add. Alongside the three chip stocks in this plan, you have enough chip exposure.

Wait for 12 Nov

Nebius: don't put more money in before its 12 Nov results. Decide afterwards using the rules in section 10.

Expectation

The model gives this plan about a 63% chance of being up in six months, and about a 7% chance of being down more than 20%. No stock is certain to do well.

Why it fell

  • Election: Democrats won both houses of Congress on 3 Nov. Opposition to new data centers was a campaign issue, and one Democrat called for pausing advanced AI.
  • Earnings: Qualcomm (phone sales −26%) and Arm gave weak outlooks on 4 Nov.
  • Rates: the Fed raised rates in September, held them at 3.75–4% in October, and markets give 65–83% odds of another rise on 9 Dec. The 10-year yield hit 5.36% in October (its highest since 2002) and is now about 4.85%.
  • Oil and inflation: Brent is about $95 because of the Iran war, and inflation is 3.4%.

What did not change

  • Nvidia sales grew 106% last quarter, and it guided to $108B next quarter.
  • AMD beat estimates on 3 Nov and guided $17B for Q4. It then fell 17% anyway.
  • Alphabet, Amazon and Microsoft all said their AI spending will rise further in 2027.
  • TSMC guided Q4 sales up 58% from a year earlier.

Prices fell because investors now pay less for each dollar of profit. Profit estimates themselves have not fallen.

2 · Timing the bottom

Half the usual bottom signals are on

Bottoms are a process. Fear and selling usually peak first. Rates, policy and a clear price rebound tend to follow weeks later. Four of the eight signals below are on. That suggests buying a first portion now and saving the rest for confirmation.

SignalStatusEvidence
History supports buying in stages. The S&P 500 has risen in the 12 months after every midterm election since 1946 (19 out of 19). Since 2011 the Nasdaq has gained an average of 22% in the year after a correction. Neither is a guarantee: both streaks happened without an aggressive Fed hiking cycle.

9 · Model E · Monte Carlo, 20,000 simulated paths

Buying in steps mainly protects the bad outcomes

I simulated the five stocks over six months 20,000 times, using today's elevated volatility and the fact that chip stocks tend to move together. Buying in steps barely changes the typical result. It does cut the chance of losing more than 20% from about 12% to 7%, at a cost of roughly $27 of average return. For your risk tolerance, that trade is worth making.

About +1% against your $400 cost. AMD has the highest expected return of the seven stocks scored (+26%). It also has the widest range of outcomes: on 5 Nov it fell 17% right after beating estimates. It trades at about 24× next year's earnings, against about 15× for Nvidia.

Nebius · ~$118

Decide after 12 Nov

About −41% against your $200 cost (I couldn't confirm the 5 Nov close; it traded $113–138 that day). Nebius fails the screen for your profile. It loses money, will spend $16–25B on data centers in 2026, carries about $8.5B of convertible debt, and gets most of its contracts from two customers.   • Hold if, on 12 Nov, revenue is about $905M or more, the full-year guidance of $3.0–3.4B stays, and there's no big new share sale.
  • Sell and move the money into the basket (Alphabet or Nvidia) if guidance is cut, more dilution is announced, or the delay spreads. Where your tax rules allow, the loss can offset gains.
  • Ignore your $200 cost when deciding. Ask whether you would buy Nebius today at $118 with your risk tolerance. The screen says no.

12 · What would change the call

What could make this plan wrong

Bearish

  • The Fed raises rates on 9 Dec and signals more. 2022 showed how that can push chip stocks down about 45%.
  • A cloud company trims 2027 AI spending. The whole AI-hardware story depends on that spending.
  • The Iran war pushes oil back above $100 and keeps inflation above 3.5%.
  • Real AI legislation passes, as opposed to proposals.
  • Rising tension around Taiwan, which hits TSMC directly and every chip maker indirectly.

Bullish

  • Nvidia beats and raises again on about 17 Nov, and the stock holds the gain.
  • Inflation cools and the odds of a December hike fall below 50%.
  • Cloud companies give specific 2027 spending numbers (one analyst estimate is around $1 trillion combined).
  • Policy turns out mild: without 60 Senate votes, the new majority can't easily pass sweeping AI rules.
  • The post-midterm pattern holds, as it has 19 times out of 19.
